Methodology & Assumptions

  • Each week, workers are paid for the full scheduled hours: regular hours at the base rate, plus hours beyond the regular week at the overtime multiplier.
  • The productivity-loss % for a given week (from the curve above) is applied to the entire scheduled week to get "effective" (actually productive) hours — this mirrors how the NECA/Business Roundtable curves express cumulative efficiency loss under sustained overtime, not just loss on the extra hours.
  • "Cost of equivalent output at straight time" asks: what would it cost, at the base hourly rate with no overtime and no productivity loss, to produce the same amount of actual work? The gap between that figure and what was actually paid is the financial impact of overtime — the premium you paid on top of the wage premium itself, caused purely by lost efficiency.
  • If effective hours in a week fall below the regular work week, that week's overtime is flagged as net-negative: the crew produced less usable output than if they had simply worked a standard week, a well-documented risk of extended sustained overtime.
  • This tool is a planning aid. It does not account for absenteeism, turnover, safety-incident costs, rework/quality costs beyond the modeled productivity loss, or schedule-specific NECA tables (5x10, 6x10, 6x12, 7x12, etc.) beyond the presets provided — adjust the curve table for your actual schedule pattern and reference data.
